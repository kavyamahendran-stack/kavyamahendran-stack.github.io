Card Fraud Pattern Analysis

Kavya Mahendran · SQL (SQLite) and Python · 2025 data · View the SQL

881,441 simulated card transactions from 999 customers, 1 Jan to 31 Dec 2025, generated with the open-source Sparkov simulator. Every figure below comes from the SQL queries in fraud_analysis.sql.

Fraud rate by hour of day

Fraud jumps from about 0.2% in the day to 3 to 5% between 22:00 and 03:59.

Fraud rate by transaction amount

Over a third of $500 to $999 payments are fraud; payments under $200 almost never are.

Fraud rate by merchant category

Online shopping, online misc and in-store grocery are the three riskiest categories.

Fraud rate by time since previous transaction

Payments within 10 minutes of the last one are 5 times riskier than those 1 to 6 hours apart.

Fraud rate by customer age

Customers aged 50 and over are targeted about twice as often as those aged 25 to 49.

Monthly fraud rate

Fraud cases stay steady; the December dip is because genuine spending nearly doubles.

Testing simple detection rules

Precision: share of alerts that were really fraud. Recall: share of all fraud cases caught. A good rule catches most fraud without flooding analysts with false alarms.

Key findings

  1. Fraud is rare (1.06% of transactions) but costly: it is 7.7% of all money spent, because the average fraud ($533) is nearly 8 times a normal payment ($69).
  2. Time of day and amount are the strongest signals. Combined, they flag 2% of transactions but catch 65% of fraud cases and 84% of fraud value.
  3. Fraud arrives in bursts: once a card is compromised, an average of 9.6 fraudulent payments are made on it within about a day.
  4. Distance from home to merchant did not separate fraud (76.8 km vs 76.4 km), so it was left out of the rules.

Recommendations

  1. Use rule R4 (amount of $200 or more at night) as a first-line alert: about 49 alerts a day, 1 in 3 of them real fraud.
  2. After the first confirmed fraud on a card, block or step up checks at once, because the rest of the burst usually follows within hours.
  3. Add velocity checks for rapid repeat payments, and extra protection for customers aged 50+.
  4. Next step: train a machine learning model on these features and compare it with the rules.

Data quality and limitations

  • Checked row counts, date range and missing values: no missing amounts, times, categories or labels.
  • Found 117,209 transaction IDs re-used across different transactions in the raw data; replaced them with unique IDs and re-checked (0 duplicates).
  • Personal details (names, addresses, card numbers) were dropped; customers carry anonymous IDs.
  • The data is simulated. 975 of 999 customers have a fraud episode, far more than in real life, so rates show patterns, not real-world levels.
